# Vertical Ranking for AI Coding and Automation Workflows

**Research date:** 2026-07-16  
**Posture:** Skeptical, evidence-driven, pilot-oriented  
**Recommendation:** **Pursue only through a narrow pilot.**

This ranking evaluates verticals specifically for **AI coding and automation workflows**, not for general demand for data. A vertical scores well only when coding agents repeatedly fail because the next correct implementation step depends on licensed, current, authoritative, externally obtainable data that can be accessed narrowly, protected from extraction, and evaluated against an objective task.

The strongest inference remains vertical rather than horizontal: **domain-rule data for regulated or high-cost automations** is a plausible wedge; generic API documentation, web search, RAG, and customer-owned internal context are not.

## Scoring convention

The same 20 dimensions are used in the accompanying `vertical-ranking.csv`:

1. Frequency of coding/automation workflows
2. Frequency of data-caused failures
3. Economic cost of poor automation
4. Proprietary data availability
5. Difficulty reproducing the data independently
6. Rights-holder identifiability
7. Rights-holder licensing willingness
8. Buyer willingness to pay
9. Temporary vs permanent access value
10. Moat preservation ability
11. Legal/regulatory complexity
12. Privacy sensitivity
13. Technical integration difficulty
14. Existing APIs/MCP availability
15. Strength of free substitutes
16. Sales-cycle length
17. Expected data spend per task
18. Potential platform margin
19. Measurability of improvement
20. 90-day pilot suitability

Scores are 1-5. Higher is better for the startup except for **legal/regulatory complexity, privacy sensitivity, technical integration difficulty, strength of free substitutes, and sales-cycle length**, where higher is worse and is inverted in the composite score. The ranking is an inference based on the prior evidence pack, not measured market research.

## Top five expanded recommendations

### 1. Compliance/sanctions KYB automation coding

- **Exact coding/automation workflow:** Build or modify a KYB onboarding automation that screens companies, officers, beneficial owners, vendors, and counterparties; resolves aliases; checks sanctions/PEP/adverse media; creates case-review artifacts; and writes auditable decision logic into a compliance SaaS or bank/fintech internal workflow.
- **Exact failure node:** The agent can implement generic matching and API plumbing but cannot determine whether a candidate entity match is authoritative, current, jurisdictionally relevant, or sufficiently supported for a compliance disposition.
- **Exact missing information:** Current sanctions and watchlist entries, PEP/adverse-media signals, ownership/control relationships, company registry records, entity aliases/transliterations, provider confidence metadata, list provenance, and jurisdiction-specific screening thresholds.
- **Likely buyer:** Compliance/KYB SaaS vendors, fintech onboarding teams, bank compliance-engineering teams, marketplace/vendor-risk teams, and crypto/fintech compliance teams using coding agents to build screening automations.
- **Likely data owner:** OpenSanctions, LSEG World-Check, Dow Jones Risk & Compliance, LexisNexis Risk Solutions, Moody's/Orbis/Bureau van Dijk, Dun & Bradstreet, company registries, and adverse-media providers.
- **Existing substitute:** Direct vendor contracts, OpenSanctions hosted API/bulk data, public OFAC/UN/EU/UK lists, government registries, KYB vendors such as Alloy/Middesk/Persona/Trulioo/Socure/Sumsub/ComplyAdvantage, or browser automation against subscribed portals.
- **Proposed access mechanism:** Entity-bounded MCP/API call that accepts subject identifiers and returns match evidence, source version, confidence, provenance, and permitted-use metadata; no bulk list export.
- **Pricing structure:** Per-screen, per-reconcile, or per-resolved coding workflow; likely cents to low dollars for OpenSanctions-like queries and higher blended prices for premium adverse-media/company-data enrichments; enterprise volume tiers.
- **Required restrictions:** No bulk extraction, no model training, no redistribution, rate limits, subject-bounded inputs, provenance retention, audit logs, human-review flags for ambiguous matches, and country/list-specific license controls.
- **Evaluation method:** Labeled KYB task suite comparing agent completion and reviewer accuracy with public lists only vs licensed minimal-access calls; measure false positives, false negatives, case-review time, provenance completeness, and cost per resolved workflow.
- **Why buyer would not go direct to data owner:** The buyer may need multiple data owners, a normalized agent-readable response, entitlement checks, fallback routing, per-task provenance, and observability-linked proof that the data call resolved a coding failure. Skeptical caveat: sophisticated banks and KYB vendors often already go direct, so the pilot must target teams without mature multi-provider screening infrastructure or agent-specific access controls.

**20-dimension narrative:** High frequency of coding workflows in fintech/compliance automation; very high frequency of data-caused failures around entity resolution and list freshness; high economic cost from onboarding friction and regulatory exposure; good proprietary data availability; difficult to reproduce due to global aliases and registries; identifiable rights holders; moderate-to-good licensing willingness because paid APIs already exist; high buyer WTP; strong temporary per-screen value; reasonable moat preservation through subject-bounded responses; moderate legal and privacy complexity; moderate integration difficulty; existing APIs and some MCP-adjacent tools; public-list substitutes are useful but incomplete; sales cycles are moderate; expected data spend per task is meaningful; platform margin can exist if broker normalizes multiple providers; improvement is measurable; 90-day pilot suitability is the best of the set.

### 2. Healthcare RCM / claims edit automation coding

- **Exact coding/automation workflow:** Build a revenue-cycle automation that validates X12 837 claims before submission, checks eligibility/benefits, applies CPT/HCPCS/ICD/modifier logic, flags prior-authorization requirements, and generates payer-specific edit explanations for RCM staff.
- **Exact failure node:** The coding agent writes syntactically valid EDI/API code but cannot encode current payer-specific edits, bundling rules, medical-policy constraints, or denial-prevention logic.
- **Exact missing information:** Payer-specific claim edits, Optum/Change-style real-time precheck responses, AMA CPT descriptors/licensing context, CMS NCCI edits and MUEs where public, plan/member eligibility, prior-auth rules, modifier guidance, and historical denial edge cases.
- **Likely buyer:** RCM vendors, clearinghouses, provider billing teams, healthtech startups building claims tools, payer/provider engineering teams, and enterprise teams using Cursor/Claude Code/Copilot to automate claim workflows.
- **Likely data owner:** Optum/Change Healthcare, AMA CPT, CMS NCCI/public CMS datasets, clearinghouses such as Availity-class networks, payers, specialty coding-content vendors, and payer-policy aggregators.
- **Existing substitute:** Direct Optum or clearinghouse APIs, payer portals, CMS public files, AMA licenses, human certified coders, billing consultants, and existing RCM software.
- **Proposed access mechanism:** PHI-minimized claim-check API/MCP wrapper that accepts only the fields required for a bounded edit question and returns edit code, rationale, source/version, and allowed downstream use. During coding, use synthetic or deidentified claim fixtures whenever possible.
- **Pricing structure:** Per claim check, per edit explanation, or per resolved implementation/test case; likely enterprise minimums plus usage tiers because HIPAA/security overhead makes pure micropayments unrealistic.
- **Required restrictions:** HIPAA BAA where PHI is involved, PHI minimization, encryption, audit logging, no training, no retention beyond contract, payer-contract compliance, least-privilege field release, and explicit source/version provenance.
- **Evaluation method:** Deidentified historical claim set with known denial/edit outcomes; compare agent-built automations using public CMS files only vs licensed precheck/edit sources; measure prevented denials, edit precision, implementation completion, and coder-review time.
- **Why buyer would not go direct to data owner:** The buyer may need routing across payer, CPT, CMS, clearinghouse, and internal policy sources while preserving agent-specific provenance and least-privilege retrieval. Skeptical caveat: large RCM buyers often already contract directly, and Optum concentration plus HIPAA compliance can consume the startup.

**20-dimension narrative:** Very high workflow relevance for claims and RCM automation; very high data-caused failure rate; very high economic cost from denials, rework, and cash-flow delay; strong proprietary data availability but concentrated; very hard to reproduce independently; identifiable rights holders; only moderate licensing willingness because incumbents prefer direct contracts; high buyer WTP; high temporary access value for claim-specific checks; decent moat preservation via bounded answers; very high legal/regulatory complexity; very high privacy sensitivity; high integration difficulty; existing APIs are real; free public CMS substitutes help but are incomplete; sales cycles are long; expected data spend per task is high; platform margin is pressured by data-owner power; improvement is measurable; 90-day pilot suitability is only moderate despite high value.

### 3. Construction/building-code compliance automation

- **Exact coding/automation workflow:** Build a plan-review or permit-precheck automation that reads project facts, jurisdiction, occupancy type, construction type, products/materials, and code edition, then generates code-compliance checks and cited remediation guidance.
- **Exact failure node:** The agent can build a rules engine or form workflow but cannot determine the controlling adopted code, local amendment, code section, exception, or product approval relevant to the project.
- **Exact missing information:** ICC model-code sections, jurisdiction-specific amendments and adopted editions, local ordinances, plan-review interpretations, product approvals/listings, fire/electrical/plumbing/mechanical standards, and citation metadata.
- **Likely buyer:** Construction proptech vendors, permit automation startups, AEC software teams, municipal plan-review modernization teams, builders/developers, and enterprise engineering teams automating internal permit workflows.
- **Likely data owner:** ICC, UpCodes, Municode/CivicPlus-class ordinance publishers, local governments, standards bodies, UL/ICC-ES/classification providers, and specialized construction-cost/code data vendors.
- **Existing substitute:** Public city/county code pages, PDFs, ICC/UpCodes subscriptions, human code consultants, municipal plan reviewers, and manual plan-check checklists.
- **Proposed access mechanism:** Section-scoped code-citation API/MCP that accepts jurisdiction/date/building facts and returns narrow excerpts, citations, amendment status, and permitted use; optionally pair with a rules-eval endpoint rather than raw code text.
- **Pricing structure:** Per code-check, per jurisdiction bundle, per permit workflow, or SaaS seat plus metered authoritative citations; likely higher value for commercial permits than residential.
- **Required restrictions:** No codebook bulk export, snippet limits, citation-only outputs when possible, jurisdiction/date scoping, no training, watermarking/provenance, and downstream-use terms for generated reports.
- **Evaluation method:** Fixed suite of permit/code questions validated by code consultants or past plan-review outcomes; measure correct citation rate, plan-review rework reduction, and agent task-completion lift over public web/PDF search.
- **Why buyer would not go direct to data owner:** The buyer needs local amendment normalization, jurisdiction routing, source versioning, and agent-readable checks rather than one code publisher contract. Skeptical caveat: data owners such as ICC/UpCodes can expose direct APIs and may not need a broker unless the broker brings distribution through coding-agent platforms.

**20-dimension narrative:** Frequent enough in proptech/AEC automation; high data-caused failures due to local adoption and amendments; high economic cost from permit delays; good proprietary data availability; hard to reproduce because authority and local context matter; rights holders are identifiable; licensing willingness is moderate; buyer WTP is good for commercial workflows; temporary access value is high for a specific permit question; moat can be protected with scoped excerpts; legal complexity is moderate; privacy sensitivity is low; integration difficulty is moderate; existing APIs exist but are uneven; public substitutes are meaningful but incomplete; sales cycle can be long with government/AEC buyers; data spend per task is moderate; platform margin could be good; improvement is measurable; 90-day pilot suitability is good if limited to one jurisdiction/code family.

### 4. Legal research automation / citation-grounded legal tech coding

- **Exact coding/automation workflow:** Build legal research, brief-checking, contract analysis, regulatory monitoring, or citation-validation automation that must retrieve authority, verify precedential status, and cite source-grounded legal text.
- **Exact failure node:** The agent produces plausible text but hallucinates citations, uses overruled or outdated authority, misses jurisdiction-specific treatment, or cannot access editorial/citator signals.
- **Exact missing information:** Full-text cases/statutes/regulations, citator treatment, headnotes/editorial taxonomy, docket materials, regulatory history, secondary sources, and licensed legal-news/current-awareness feeds.
- **Likely buyer:** Legal tech vendors, law-firm innovation teams, corporate legal departments, regtech builders, and enterprise teams coding internal legal-research automations.
- **Likely data owner:** Thomson Reuters/Westlaw, LexisNexis, Bloomberg Law, vLex/Fastcase-class providers, court docket providers, legal publishers, and public-law projects for free baselines.
- **Existing substitute:** Direct legal-research subscriptions, Lexis/Westlaw APIs or AI products, CourtListener and official public sources, law-firm knowledge bases, browser automation using a subscribed account, and human legal researchers.
- **Proposed access mechanism:** Citation-bounded retrieval/citator API that returns authority status, snippets, jurisdiction/date metadata, and license constraints rather than broad corpus access.
- **Pricing structure:** Per citation validation, per research question, or enterprise minimum plus usage; premium sources likely require contract-based pricing rather than open micropayments.
- **Required restrictions:** No corpus dumping, no model training without explicit rights, snippet/quote limits, source display requirements, user entitlement checks, audit logs, and jurisdiction-specific disclaimers.
- **Evaluation method:** Citation accuracy benchmark; compare hallucination rate, correct authority treatment, source recall, and lawyer-review time with and without licensed access.
- **Why buyer would not go direct to data owner:** A broker can normalize multiple sources, enforce minimal access inside coding-agent traces, and pay only for citation checks that resolve a workflow. Skeptical caveat: the main legal publishers have strong brands, contracts, and their own AI products, so direct sourcing is a serious substitute.

**20-dimension narrative:** High frequency in legal-tech coding; high data-caused failure rate around citations and source authority; high economic cost from wrong legal outputs; strong proprietary data availability; difficult to reproduce because citators/editorial treatment are proprietary; rights holders are identifiable; licensing willingness is moderate but controlled; buyer WTP is high; temporary access value is high for citation checks; moat preservation is plausible through snippet/citation gating; legal complexity is high; privacy is moderate; integration difficulty is moderate; APIs and AI products exist; free public-law substitutes are useful but incomplete; sales cycles are long; data spend per task is meaningful; margin may be constrained by publisher pricing; improvement is measurable; 90-day pilot can work on citation validation rather than broad research.

### 5. Logistics rate/routing automation

- **Exact coding/automation workflow:** Build a freight quoting, lane-pricing, carrier-selection, routing, surcharge, delivery-window, or exception-management automation for a 3PL, freight forwarder, shipper, or logistics SaaS.
- **Exact failure node:** The agent writes a routing/rate-shopping workflow but lacks current lane benchmarks, carrier availability, tariff/surcharge rules, mode constraints, sailing/flight schedules, or quote APIs needed for correct decisions.
- **Exact missing information:** Current freight rates, quote/booking availability, carrier schedules, fuel/accessorial surcharges, lane benchmarks, port/terminal updates, capacity indicators, and carrier/service constraints.
- **Likely buyer:** Logistics SaaS vendors, freight forwarders, 3PLs, shipper engineering teams, procurement/logistics automation teams, and agent platforms serving supply-chain customers.
- **Likely data owner:** Freightos/WebCargo, DAT Freight & Analytics, FreightWaves SONAR, project44/FourKites-style visibility networks, carriers, GDS-like transport systems, ports/terminal operators, and TMS vendors.
- **Existing substitute:** Direct carrier APIs, TMS data, Freightos/DAT/SONAR subscriptions, manual quoting desks, public schedules, and customer-owned historical shipments.
- **Proposed access mechanism:** Lane-, mode-, and TTL-scoped API/MCP call returning quote, benchmark, schedule, confidence, cache expiry, source, and usage limits.
- **Pricing structure:** Per quote, per lane benchmark, per routing decision, or subscription plus metered premium data; cache-aware pricing is important because freshness decays quickly.
- **Required restrictions:** No systematic scraping or bulk lane reconstruction, cache TTLs, no redistribution, rate limits, account-specific entitlements, provenance, and quote-use restrictions.
- **Evaluation method:** Backtest against historical shipments and live quote outcomes; measure quote accuracy, margin leakage, route-choice quality, and agent completion rate versus static/public data.
- **Why buyer would not go direct to data owner:** A broker can normalize heterogeneous carrier/benchmark sources and expose a task-scoped agent endpoint with fallback routing. Skeptical caveat: large logistics buyers already integrate directly with TMS, carrier, and rate providers, so the pilot should target agent-built automations in teams without mature data normalization.

**20-dimension narrative:** Good coding-workflow frequency in logistics automation; high data-caused failure rate for rates and schedules; high economic cost from bad quotes/routing; proprietary data availability is good but fragmented; independent reproduction is only moderately hard because customers have partial histories; rights holders are identifiable but numerous; licensing willingness is moderate; buyer WTP is good; temporary access value is very high because rates expire; moat preservation is harder because benchmarks can be reconstructed; legal complexity and privacy are moderate-to-low; integration difficulty is moderate; APIs exist; public substitutes are partial; sales cycles are moderate; expected data spend per task is moderate; platform margin is moderate; improvement is measurable; 90-day pilot suitability is good with one mode and one lane family.
